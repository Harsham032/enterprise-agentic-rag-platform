## Background

The proportion of people with persistent symptoms two years after SARS-CoV-2 infection, and the contribution of infection itself relative to background symptom prevalence, remain uncertain because many studies lack uninfected comparators.

## Methods

We followed 6,428 adults with polymerase chain reaction confirmed SARS-CoV-2 infection and 6,428 contemporaneously recruited uninfected controls matched on age, sex, region, vaccination status and pre-pandemic comorbidity. Symptoms were collected by validated questionnaire at 6, 12 and 24 months.

## Results

At 24 months, 11.4 percent of the infected group and 5.9 percent of controls reported at least one moderate or severe symptom in the fatigue, cognitive or autonomic domains, an excess of 5.5 percentage points (95 percent confidence interval 4.4 to 6.6). The excess fell from 14.8 percentage points at 6 months. Excess prevalence was concentrated among those hospitalised during acute infection (18.1 percentage points) compared with those managed in the community (3.6 percentage points). Reinfection during follow-up was associated with a 2.2 percentage point increase in symptom prevalence.

## Conclusions

Excess symptom burden attributable to infection declined substantially between 6 and 24 months but did not resolve. Using a matched uninfected comparator reduced the apparent prevalence of persistent symptoms by roughly half relative to an uncontrolled estimate.
