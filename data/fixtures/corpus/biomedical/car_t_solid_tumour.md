## Background

Chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy has transformed outcomes in B cell malignancies but has shown limited activity in solid tumours, where antigen heterogeneity and an immunosuppressive microenvironment restrict persistence.

## Methods

Thirty-one patients with claudin-18.2 positive advanced gastric adenocarcinoma who had progressed on at least two prior lines received autologous claudin-18.2 directed CAR T cells across four dose levels following lymphodepletion. Primary endpoints were safety and determination of the recommended Phase 2 dose.

## Results

The objective response rate was 38.7 percent (12 of 31) and the disease control rate was 71.0 percent. Median progression-free survival was 5.6 months and median overall survival was 9.8 months. Cytokine release syndrome occurred in 27 patients and was grade 3 or higher in 4. One treatment-related death from gastric mucosal injury occurred at the highest dose level, which was not taken forward. CAR T cell expansion peaked at a median of day 11 and persistence beyond day 90 was detected in 9 of 31 patients.

## Conclusions

Claudin-18.2 directed CAR T cells produced objective responses in a heavily pretreated gastric cancer population, with on-target off-tumour gastric toxicity as the principal dose-limiting risk. Persistence remained short in most patients.
