## Background

Carbapenem-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae is a leading cause of difficult-to-treat bloodstream infection. Trends in resistance mechanism prevalence inform empirical therapy choices.

## Methods

We analysed 14,602 Klebsiella pneumoniae bloodstream isolates collected between 2014 and 2022 from 61 hospitals in 19 countries. Susceptibility was determined by broth microdilution and carbapenemase genes were identified by polymerase chain reaction and whole genome sequencing on a systematic subsample of 3,114 isolates.

## Results

Overall carbapenem resistance rose from 18.4 percent in 2014 to 31.7 percent in 2022. Among sequenced resistant isolates the proportion carrying blaKPC fell from 62 percent to 39 percent while blaNDM rose from 14 percent to 36 percent and blaOXA-48-like remained stable near 22 percent. Thirty-day mortality was 41.2 percent among carbapenem-resistant infections versus 19.8 percent among susceptible infections (adjusted odds ratio 2.14, 95 percent confidence interval 1.87 to 2.45).

## Conclusions

Carbapenem resistance increased steadily over nine years and the dominant mechanism shifted from KPC toward NDM. Because the newer beta-lactam beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations are inactive against metallo-beta-lactamases, this shift has direct implications for empirical regimen selection.
