## Background

Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists produce substantial weight loss, but the trajectory of weight regain after treatment stops has not been characterised in a randomised withdrawal design.

## Methods

After a 20-week open-label lead-in during which 1,004 adults with a body mass index of at least 30 received weekly semaglutide-class therapy, 812 participants who achieved at least 5 percent weight loss were randomised 1:1 to continue active treatment or to switch to placebo for 52 weeks. The primary endpoint was percentage change in body weight from randomisation to week 52.

## Results

Mean weight change from randomisation to week 52 was minus 4.1 percent in the continuation group and plus 11.6 percent in the withdrawal group, a treatment difference of 15.7 percentage points (95 percent confidence interval 14.2 to 17.2, p less than 0.001). At week 52 the withdrawal group had regained a mean of 68 percent of the weight lost during lead-in. Systolic blood pressure and triglyceride improvements also reverted toward baseline in the withdrawal group. Gastrointestinal adverse events were more frequent with continued treatment (41.2 percent versus 12.8 percent).

## Conclusions

Most weight lost during the lead-in was regained within 52 weeks of discontinuation, together with the associated cardiometabolic improvements. These data support treating obesity as a chronic condition requiring sustained therapy rather than a finite course.
