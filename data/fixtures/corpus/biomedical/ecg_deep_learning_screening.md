## Background

Convolutional models applied to the 12-lead electrocardiogram can detect reduced left ventricular ejection fraction, but reported performance has come largely from single-centre retrospective data and external generalisation is uncertain.

## Methods

A convolutional neural network was trained on 412,000 paired electrocardiogram and echocardiogram studies from one academic health system and evaluated without retraining on 96,300 paired studies from three geographically separate systems. The outcome was an ejection fraction at or below 40 percent. Subgroup performance was assessed by sex, by age band and by self-reported race.

## Results

Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve was 0.93 in internal testing and fell to 0.87, 0.85 and 0.89 across the three external systems. Sensitivity at the pre-specified operating threshold ranged from 0.79 to 0.86. Expected calibration error rose from 0.021 internally to between 0.058 and 0.094 externally, and recalibration on 2,000 local studies reduced it to below 0.03 in all three systems. Performance was 0.04 to 0.06 lower in women than in men across every site.

## Conclusions

Discrimination degraded modestly and calibration degraded substantially on external validation. Site-level recalibration recovered calibration at low cost, and the persistent sex gap warrants investigation before deployment.
