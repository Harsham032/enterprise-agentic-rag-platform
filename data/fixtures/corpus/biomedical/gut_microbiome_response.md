## Background

Retrospective studies have linked gut microbiome composition to checkpoint inhibitor response in melanoma, but findings have not replicated consistently across cohorts and geographies.

## Methods

We prospectively collected baseline stool samples from 508 patients with unresectable stage III or IV melanoma starting anti-PD-1 therapy at 14 centres on three continents. Shotgun metagenomic sequencing was performed centrally. The primary analysis tested the association between a pre-specified 12-species signature derived from earlier cohorts and 12-month progression-free survival.

## Results

The pre-specified signature was not associated with 12-month progression-free survival in the full cohort (hazard ratio 0.91, 95 percent confidence interval 0.74 to 1.12). A significant interaction with geography was observed (p for interaction 0.003): the signature was associated with response in the European subcohort (hazard ratio 0.63, 0.45 to 0.88) but not in the North American or Australian subcohorts. Alpha diversity was not associated with response in any subcohort.

## Conclusions

A previously reported microbiome signature did not replicate in a prospective multicentre cohort, and its apparent effect was confined to one geography. Region-specific dietary and reference-database differences are plausible explanations and must be addressed before microbiome-based patient selection is considered.
