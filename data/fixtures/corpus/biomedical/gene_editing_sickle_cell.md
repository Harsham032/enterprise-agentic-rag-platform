## Background

Severe sickle cell disease is caused by a single point mutation in the beta-globin gene. Reactivating fetal haemoglobin expression by disrupting the BCL11A erythroid enhancer has been proposed as a curative strategy, but durability beyond two years is not well characterised.

## Methods

We enrolled 45 patients aged 12 to 35 years with at least two severe vaso-occlusive crises per year in an open-label single-arm study. Autologous CD34-positive cells were edited ex vivo at the BCL11A erythroid enhancer and reinfused following busulfan conditioning. The primary endpoint was freedom from severe vaso-occlusive crisis for at least 12 consecutive months. Patients were followed for a median of 30 months.

## Results

Forty-three of 45 patients (95.6 percent) met the primary endpoint. Mean fetal haemoglobin rose from 5.2 percent of total haemoglobin at baseline to 41.3 percent at month 6 and was 39.8 percent at month 24. Total haemoglobin increased by a mean of 3.4 grams per decilitre. Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 38 patients and were predominantly attributable to busulfan conditioning; there were two cases of prolonged thrombocytopenia and no malignancies.

## Conclusions

Editing of the BCL11A erythroid enhancer produced durable fetal haemoglobin induction and near-elimination of severe vaso-occlusive crises over a median of 30 months. Conditioning toxicity, not the editing step, drove the adverse event profile.
