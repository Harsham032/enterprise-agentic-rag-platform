## Background

No vaccine currently prevents progression from latent tuberculosis infection to active pulmonary disease in adults. BCG provides limited protection in this population.

## Methods

We randomised 3,612 adults aged 18 to 50 with interferon-gamma release assay confirmed latent infection and no evidence of active disease 1:1 to two doses of a subunit vaccine or placebo across sites in three high-burden countries. The primary endpoint was microbiologically confirmed pulmonary tuberculosis through month 36.

## Results

Primary endpoint cases occurred in 27 of 1,806 vaccine recipients and 54 of 1,806 placebo recipients, giving a vaccine efficacy of 50.1 percent (95 percent confidence interval 21.4 to 68.2, p equals 0.003). Efficacy was 54.8 percent in participants under 35 and 39.2 percent in those aged 35 and above, though the interaction was not statistically significant. Injection site reactions were reported by 61 percent of vaccine recipients versus 19 percent of placebo recipients; serious adverse events were balanced between groups.

## Conclusions

The vaccine halved progression to microbiologically confirmed pulmonary tuberculosis over 36 months in adults with latent infection. The confidence interval is wide and a Phase 3 trial powered for subgroup effects and longer follow-up is required.
