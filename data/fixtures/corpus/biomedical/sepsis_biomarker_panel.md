## Background

Distinguishing bacterial sepsis from non-infectious systemic inflammation at emergency department presentation remains difficult. Procalcitonin alone has shown inconsistent discrimination in undifferentiated populations.

## Methods

We prospectively enrolled 2,188 adults presenting with suspected infection at four emergency departments. A five-protein panel comprising procalcitonin, presepsin, interleukin-6, soluble urokinase plasminogen activator receptor and pentraxin-3 was measured on the index blood draw. The reference standard was adjudicated bacterial sepsis by two blinded clinicians. The cohort was split into a derivation set of 1,458 and a temporally held-out validation set of 730.

## Results

In the held-out validation set the panel achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve of 0.89 (95 percent confidence interval 0.86 to 0.92) compared with 0.78 (0.74 to 0.82) for procalcitonin alone. At a threshold set for 95 percent sensitivity, specificity was 61 percent and the negative predictive value was 97.8 percent. Calibration was adequate with an expected-to-observed ratio of 1.04.

## Conclusions

A five-protein panel improved discrimination of bacterial sepsis over procalcitonin alone in an undifferentiated emergency population. The high negative predictive value suggests a potential role in ruling out bacterial sepsis, though a randomised implementation study is required before clinical adoption.
