## Item 1. Business

Northwind Energy Corporation develops, owns and operates onshore wind and utility-scale solar generation assets across the upper Midwest and the Texas panhandle. As of December 31, 2024 the Company owned interests in 34 operating projects with 4,180 megawatts of net installed capacity, of which 3,020 megawatts is wind and 1,160 megawatts is solar. Approximately 78 percent of expected 2025 generation is contracted under power purchase agreements with a weighted average remaining term of 9.4 years. The Company sells the balance of its output into the wholesale markets operated by the Midcontinent Independent System Operator and the Electric Reliability Council of Texas.

The Company's development pipeline totalled 6,900 megawatts at year end, of which 1,450 megawatts had reached the advanced stage, meaning interconnection queue position, site control and a signed offtake term sheet were all in place. Northwind employed 1,240 people at December 31, 2024, of whom 410 worked in field operations and maintenance.

## Item 1A. Risk Factors

Our generation revenue depends on weather conditions that we do not control. Wind speeds in the upper Midwest during 2024 were approximately 6 percent below the ten-year median, which reduced wind generation volumes by 412 gigawatt hours relative to our long-term production estimate. A sustained period of below-median resource availability would reduce revenue and could cause us to fall short of the availability guarantees embedded in several of our power purchase agreements.

We depend on a concentrated set of turbine suppliers. Two manufacturers supplied 84 percent of the turbines in our operating fleet, and one of those two supplied every turbine installed at our four largest wind projects. A prolonged production interruption, insolvency or trade restriction affecting either supplier would delay our construction programme and increase the cost of replacement components. We have not qualified an alternative supplier for the 5.6 megawatt platform used in our 2025 construction plan.

Interconnection queue delays represent a material constraint on our growth. The median time from queue entry to an executed interconnection agreement in our principal markets extended to 44 months in 2024 from 31 months in 2021. Projects that cannot secure timely interconnection may lose their offtake agreements or their eligibility for production tax credits.

We are exposed to merchant price risk on the uncontracted portion of our generation. A one dollar per megawatt hour change in realised merchant prices would have changed 2024 revenue by approximately 8.4 million dollars.

Our debt agreements contain financial covenants. At December 31, 2024 our consolidated net leverage ratio was 4.1 times adjusted EBITDA against a covenant ceiling of 5.0 times. A decline in generation volumes or realised prices could reduce headroom under this covenant.

## Item 7. Management's Discussion and Analysis of Financial Condition and Results of Operations

Total revenue for 2024 was 1,284.6 million dollars, an increase of 4.2 percent from 1,232.8 million dollars in 2023. The increase reflects a full year of operation at the Prairie Creek and Sandhill solar projects, which added 96.3 million dollars of contracted revenue, partially offset by a 41.7 million dollar decline in wind revenue caused by below-median wind resource.

Cost of operations rose to 438.2 million dollars from 402.9 million dollars, an increase of 8.8 percent. The increase was driven by higher scheduled major-component maintenance on the legacy wind fleet and by a 14.6 million dollar increase in property insurance premiums following two consecutive years of severe convective storm losses in the Texas panhandle.

Adjusted EBITDA was 742.1 million dollars compared with 731.4 million dollars in 2023. Net income attributable to common shareholders was 118.9 million dollars, or 1.42 dollars per diluted share, compared with 143.2 million dollars, or 1.71 dollars per diluted share, in 2023. The decline reflects higher interest expense following the refinancing of 600 million dollars of project debt at a weighted average rate of 6.3 percent, up from 4.1 percent on the retired facilities.

Capital expenditure was 612.0 million dollars in 2024 compared with 548.5 million dollars in 2023, substantially all of which related to the construction of the Two Rivers and Caprock projects. We expect capital expenditure of between 700 and 780 million dollars in 2025.

Cash and cash equivalents were 214.8 million dollars at December 31, 2024. Undrawn capacity under the revolving credit facility was 450.0 million dollars.
